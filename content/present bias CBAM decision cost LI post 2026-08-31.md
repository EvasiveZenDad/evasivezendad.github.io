"Now is not the right time to do this." I have said it, you have probably said it too, and it is also how bad decisions with real, long-term costs get made.

David Laibson, the Harvard economist who named "present bias," showed that we discount future pain against present gain in predictable, measurable ways. What is less studied, in my experience, is the cost of ignoring 2nd and 3rd order consequences. I found 2 live examples today. One will be unfolding over years, the other can be fixed immediately.

The US is rolling back clean energy policy, which NRDC estimates could cost 390-540 GW of clean power capacity that would otherwise have been built. This means more burning of fossil fuels that generate more CO2.

The direct economic impact in EU trade relationships is probably limited. The EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) taxes the embedded carbon in goods imported into the EU, but since the US does not export much of the CBAM-covered precursor materials (steel, aluminium) to Europe directly, this does not look like a big problem on the surface.

However, CBAM also tracks where the raw materials in an imported good were actually produced, not just where the good was assembled. Canada exports products made from steel and aluminium to the EU too, much of it cleaner than the US equivalent because of Canadian hydropower (electricity is roughly 40% of the cost of producing aluminium, an energy-intensive process by nature). A Canadian product built with US steel will carry a higher carbon cost than the same product built with Canadian steel, even though the policy that caused this never touched Canada directly, and that is the 2nd order effect nobody is watching. My read is that this takes years to become visible and probably longer to unwind, though that is not yet a confirmed finding, just where the mechanism points.

On the other hand, Frances Frei and Anne Morriss dropped a Fixable episode this morning where Frances asks the questions and Anne is the guest for once (Toolkit: Coach like a pro). This conversation surfaces the critical idea that coaching your team now is what makes them fast and capable later, and coaching in the moment is always more effective than coaching after the fact. "Now is not the right time to invest in this" has a real cost to your business too, but it might be less visible and more destructive than a tax on imported aluminium goods.

Both examples cost real money and have real downsides, and both are avoidable right up until the point where they are not, which is exactly the kind of pattern my neurospicy 🐿️🧠 brain is built to catch early. The 2nd and 3rd order effect does not check whether you are ready for it. It just arrives late, after the moment where you could have done something about it has already passed.
